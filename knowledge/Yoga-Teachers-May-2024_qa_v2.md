**Acarya Siddhartha** _29/05/24, 3:35:16 PM_:
Two points that are important for all to understand--

1. Dharma: It is not that this definition of dharma is for yoga, and the definition of dharma as "duty" is for another philosophy and both are fine, they sit side by side, both legitimate. Rather, dharma is a concept that conveys transcendentality. It is transcendental, universal-- it applies to all human beings. Whereas everyone has different duties. So dharma cannot mean "duty". The meaning expressed in yoga is the only correct meaning. 

The usages of dharma as 'religion' or as 'duty' are misguided, incorrect usages. Dharma as a universal, defining characteristic that makes something what it is-- this is the proper meaning and it is etymologically based. It is the शाब्दिक अर्थ । This is how the word is derived from धृ-धातु । 

2. Animals follow animal dharma appropriately because they are animals and so, having finite desires, they do not abuse the four aspects of animal dharma. **Acarya Siddhartha** _05/06/24, 11:28:48 PM_:
Regarding "quantitative difference" versus "qualitative difference", the main thing to make clear is that in "quantitative difference", all the jiivas being compared have the quality to some extent-- some have more of it, and some less. For example, with buddhi, all have buddhi to some extent. Some have more and some have less.  But ALL have it to some extent. So we cannot justify jati-bhed on this point, because all are having the guna to a lesser or greater extent.

Whereas in "qualitative difference", some of the jiivas have it, whereas others do not have it at all. It is a matter of all vs none. With the guna of having infinite desire, all humans have it, whereas no animal has it at all. It is not that animals have it to some extent; they do not have it at all. So here it is on/off, or all/none. This is the reason underlying jati bhed.

People are born having the faculty of intellect, and by birth some have more of it and some have less. With regard to its development and use, there are two factors:
1. Intellect is a tool that can be used or abused. That is, depending upon whether one is guided by manava dharma or (abuse of) pashu dharma in one's life, one may or may not make proper use of one's intellect. So one may be born with a very sharp intellect, but be guided by crude samskaras to use this intellect for negative purposes. There is no shortage of such people. 2. Regarding the effect of sadhana on one's intellect: Sadhana has two effects on intellect. (a) Firstly, sadhana will guide one to use this tool of intellect in the proper way, as su-buddhi. **Acarya Siddhartha** _21/06/24, 10:54:23 AM_:
Regarding amount of food to be taken: Fruits and vegetables are naturally much less calorie-dense than any animal product. They are also less calorie-dense than grains and beans. As a result, if one is eating mostly raw fruits and vegetables as I do, then one will be eating what appears as a much larger quantity of food. Fruits and vegetables are highly nutrient dense: they contain literally thousands of health-supporting anti-oxidants and phyto-chemicals. But these are micronutrients. That means they do not contain any calories. Macronutrients (carbohydrates, protein, fat) are what contain calories. That is the difference between macronutrients and micronutrients. So fruits and vegetables contain thousands of micronutrients, and a lot of water, and relatively few calories. As a result, one needs to eat more of them. For lunch I may eat 5 applies, 5 oranges, a large bunch of grapes, and a bunch if bananas. To people who are not used to eating this way it sounds like a lot of food. There is absolutely no need to count calories. Your body will tell you when you've eaten a sufficient amount. Let it be a natural process. People do not need to count calories or measure their food. They should simply eat their food, and the rest will take care of itself. Reassure your students that this is the best food and the best way to live a human life.

A question has arisen about water and ice, and I thought I would ask your thoughts about it.

The question regards whether ice is ksiti tattva or jala tattva. To me it seems to have characteristics of both.

We talk about the principle that as tamoguna continues to increase its effect on the citta of bhuma man, we get the successive emergence of the pancha bhuta, from akasha tattva to ksiti tattva. In the process of this progressive sthulikarana:

1. The intermolecular distance within the component parts of each tattva gradually decreases as the tattva transforms from one tattva into the next.
2. With the emergence of each successive tattva we get the addition of another tanmatra which is conveyed within it. Starting from just shabda tanmatra in Akasha tattva, and progressing to all five tanmatras in ksiti tattva.
3. With the emergency of ksiti tattva we get another characteristic which is unique to this tattva, i.e. not present in the other four. And that is its ability to remain separate from other otherwise similar vastus. So even if we put two stones next to each other in physical contact with each other, they do not merge together but remain as separate stones. Whereas with water, fire, air, and space, this is not the case. if we put some water next to another quantity of water they will become one mass of water, unless separated by a container made of ksiti tattva. The same is true of fire, air and space.

So regarding ice: of the three above-described variables, the characteristics of ice are such that it does not clearly fit in as jala tattva, and nor as ksiti tattva.

1. The intermolectular distance within the component parts of ice actually increase as liquid water transforms into ice. Hence ice is actually less dense than water, which is why ice floats in water whereas stones sink in water. In contrast to ice, the intermolecular distance within the component parts of stone is less than that of water, hence stones are denser than water.
2. In ksiti tattva there are all five tanmatras including the final one, gandha tanmatra, but ice would seem to be similar to liquid water in the sense of not having any gandha.
3. Ice does possess however this third characteristic which is unique to ksiti tattva: if we place two blocks of ice one next to the other in physical contact, they will remain as twp separate blocks of ice.
